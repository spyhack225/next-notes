Dictation

Talk into anything.

Most of what you write in a day is short and unglamorous — a reply, a commit message, a note to yourself. Typing it is the slow part.

Hold a key and say it instead. The filler comes out, the punctuation goes in, and the finished sentence is inserted where you were already working. It learns the names and jargon you actually use, so it stops mangling them.

You said um so i need to send the report by friday no wait make that thursday It wrote So I need to send the report by Thursday.

Meetings

Show up empty-handed.

Speechify sees what's on your calendar and quietly starts when the meeting does. It asks first, and one click skips any meeting you'd rather it stayed out of.

It hears your microphone and what comes out of your speakers as two separate things, which is how the transcript knows who said what without anything joining the call.

When you stop, you get a summary, the decisions, and who owes what by when.

  • Starts and stops on its own, or never — your choice, per meeting
  • Tells the people in the room apart
  • Summary, decisions, action items and open questions
  • Works with Zoom, Meet, Teams, or a conversation at a desk
  • Can offer to write the doc and send the follow-up, if you let it

The difference

It runs on your machine.
That's the whole product.

Every other tool in this category ships your voice to a server and asks you to trust what happens next. Speechify does the transcription and the writing on the Mac in front of you.

  • No account, no subscription, no cloud
  • Recordings are deleted once the notes are written, unless you ask to keep them
  • Your calendar is read, never written
  • It cannot send an email or create a document until you press the button
  • Open source, so none of the above has to be taken on faith

Get it

Build it in two minutes.

There's no signed release yet, so you build it yourself. If you have Xcode installed, that's two commands:

git clone https://github.com/spyhack225/speechify-site.git
cd speechify-site && make install

It compiles, installs to your Applications folder and launches. macOS will ask for the microphone and a couple of permissions on first run, and the app walks you through them.
